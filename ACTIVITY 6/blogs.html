Arithmetic: order of operations

When an expression has several operations, do multiplication and division before addition and subtraction. Work from left to right when operations have equal priority.

Example: 3 + 4 × 2 = 3 + 8 = 11. Multiply first.

Algebra: solve for a variable

Keep an equation balanced by doing the same operation to both sides. Undo addition or subtraction first, then undo multiplication or division.

Example: 2x + 3 = 11. Subtract 3: 2x = 8. Divide by 2: x = 4.

Geometry: area of a rectangle

Area measures the space inside a flat shape. For a rectangle, multiply its length by its width.

Example: A rectangle 6 cm long and 4 cm wide has area 6 × 4 = 24 cm².

Statistics: find the mean

The mean, or average, is the total of the values divided by how many values there are.

Example: For 4, 6, and 8, the mean is (4 + 6 + 8) ÷ 3 = 6.

Trigonometry: sine, cosine, and tangent

In a right triangle, these ratios connect an angle to its sides: sine = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, cosine = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, and tangent = opposite ÷ adjacent.

Example: If the opposite side is 3 and the hypotenuse is 5, the sine of the angle is 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6.

Calculus: a simple derivative

A derivative describes how quickly a function changes. For a power of x, multiply by the exponent and reduce the exponent by one.

Example: If f(x) = x², then f′(x) = 2x. At x = 3, the rate of change is 6.
